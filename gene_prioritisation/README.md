# DENND1B locus gene prioritisation exercise

## Question
Why was DENND1B prioritised at the selected canine obesity locus,
and how strongly does the available evidence distinguish it from
alternative candidate genes?

## Purpose
A retrospective applicant exercise demonstrating evidence assessment
and research judgement. The published conclusion is already known.

## Scope
- One canine obesity-associated locus.
- Published interval definitions and assembly-compatible annotation.
- A documented candidate list and qualitative evidence comparison.
- Existing supplementary tables S3–S7 and the associated paper.
- Existing HYPOMAP results used only as supporting expression evidence.

## Deliverables
- Locus definition and candidate list.
- One evidence table.
- One-page interpretation with questions for the supervisor.
- A simple locus figure or coordinate table if useful.

## Boundaries
No new GWAS, imputation, fine-mapping, experimental validation,
general-purpose pipeline or numerical causal scoring system.
Do not assume DENND1B and LHX9 are the complete candidate set.

## Current status
Published-source evidence review drafted:
- Canine locus, assembly and published seven-gene candidate set recorded.
- S5-S7 evidence coverage compared across candidates.
- DENND1B regulatory, patient and functional evidence assessed.
- Supervisor discussion brief drafted.

Independent annotation-based verification of candidate coordinates
remains incomplete. No association or functional experiments were rerun.

## Associated publication
Wallis et al. (2025).
Canine genome-wide association study identifies DENND1B as an
obesity gene in dogs and humans.
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads2145

## Working rule
Each task must clarify the scientific question, improve evidence
reliability or contribute directly to the supervisor-facing package.
